## 🕒 Minute 1:55 to 3:45 — SLIDE 4: Shocks, Recovery & Structural Gaps
*(Click to Slide 4. Speak with confidence. Point to each panel and chip as you speak.)*

> "Slide 4 answers three questions.  
> 
> **Question 1: Who was hit hardest by the two big shocks?**  
> * In **2008**, **Spain** was hit hardest `[point to 2008 panel]`. Unemployment went from **8% to 26%** because the housing and construction bubble burst. The US went from **4.6% to almost 10%**.  
> * But look at **Germany**. Almost **no increase!** Germany used **Kurzarbeit** `[say: KOORTZ-ar-bite]`. Companies cut hours instead of firing people, and the government paid part of the salary.  
> * In **2020**, the **US** had the fastest jump: **14.7%** in April. But **South Africa** was hit hardest in total: a record **34%**. `[point to COVID panel]`  
> 
> **Question 2: Who recovered?** `[point to green, red, and orange chips]`  
> * The **US, Germany, and Japan** came back to normal fast.  
> * **South Africa never recovered.** Spain took more than ten years, and it is still above **11%**.  
> 
> **Question 3: Why are Spain and South Africa so high?**  
> * Our answer is: **it is structural, not cyclical.** `[point to red badge]` Cyclical unemployment goes away when the economy grows. Here, it stays high even in good years.  
> * In **Spain**, the labor market has two tiers. Older workers have safe, permanent jobs. Young workers get short contracts and are fired first. Also, ex-construction workers lacked modern skills.  
> * In **South Africa**, the cause is the history of **Apartheid** `[say: uh-PAR-tide]`. Black workers were forced to live far from city jobs, so transport is too expensive. Also, schools give little technical training: a **skills mismatch**."
