## 🕒 Minute 5:45 to 7:45 — SLIDE 6: Paper Summary — David Autor (2015)
*(Click to Slide 6. Speak with energy; tell the ATM story like an interesting story.)*

> "Slide 6 explains David Autor's famous 2015 paper: *'Why Are There Still So Many Jobs?'*.  
> 
> Autor explains two big reasons why machines do not destroy work:  
> 
> ### 1. Machines Substitute vs. Machines Complement
> * When people talk about technology, they only talk about **machine substitution**. This means a machine replaces a human doing a simple, repetitive task—like a computer doing arithmetic, or a robot painting a car.  
> * But people forget about **machine complementarity** `[say: com-ple-men-TAIR-i-tee]`! This means a machine acts as a tool that makes human workers **faster, smarter, and more productive**.  
> 
> Let me give you Autor's famous example: **The ATM machine in banking.**  
> * In the 1980s, banks introduced ATM machines to give cash. Everyone thought all human bank tellers would lose their jobs.  
> * But guess what happened? Between 1980 and 2010, **the number of bank tellers in the US actually WENT UP!**  
> * Why? Because ATMs made opening a bank branch much cheaper. So banks opened **40% more branches**!  
> * And bank tellers didn't just count cash anymore. Computers did that for them. Instead, tellers became relationship managers: talking to customers, solving problems, and selling home loans. The machine helped them do higher-value work!  
> 
> ### 2. The O-Ring Theory and Higher Income
> * Autor uses an economics idea called the **O-Ring Theory** `[created by Michael Kremer]`.  
> * Think of a team project: if machines do all the routine, boring steps quickly and without mistakes, the remaining human steps—like making decisions, creative thinking, and talking to clients—become **much more valuable, not less!**  
> * Also, when automation makes everyday goods cheaper, families save money. What do they do with that saved money? They spend it on healthcare, travel, restaurants, education, and entertainment. This creates millions of new jobs!"
