> The first big lesson from this chart is that **different countries have very different normal rates of unemployment**.  
> 
> * Look at **Japan** at the bottom `[point to purple line]`: Japan's unemployment is always very low, between 2% and 5%. Japanese companies traditionally try hard to keep their workers.

> * But now, look at the top lines: **Spain** `[point to red line]` and **South Africa** `[point to orange line]`. Their unemployment is shockingly high! Spain is often between 15% and 25%, and South Africa is between 20% and 34%. They face chronic job problems."

## 🕒 Minute 2:15 to 3:45 — SLIDE 4: Shocks, Recovery & Why Spain and South Africa Suffer
*(Click to Slide 4. Speak with confidence and clear pauses.)*

> "Slide 4 answers the questions about this chart.  
> 
> **First, which countries were hit hardest by the two big shocks?**  
> * In the **2008 Great Recession**, **Spain was hit hardest**. Spanish unemployment jumped from 8% in 2007 to an extreme peak of **26% in 2013**! Their construction bubble collapsed. The US also jumped from 4.6% to almost 10%.  
> * But notice **Germany**: Germany had almost **zero increase** in unemployment! Why? Because Germany used a government program called **Kurzarbeit** `[say: KOORTZ-ar-bite]`. Instead of firing workers, companies reduced working hours, and the government paid part of the lost salary. This protected jobs!  
> * In the **2020 COVID shock**, the United States had the fastest jump, reaching 14.7% in April 2020. But in total numbers, **South Africa was hit hardest**, reaching a world record of **34% unemployment**.  
> 
> **Second, which countries recovered back to normal?**  
> * The US, Germany, and Japan recovered quickly to normal low levels.  
> * But South Africa **never recovered**; its unemployment kept climbing. Spain took more than ten years to slowly come down, and is still above 12%.  
> 
> **Third, why is unemployment in Spain and South Africa so high? Is it cyclical or structural?**  
> * Our clear answer is: **It is STRUCTURAL unemployment!**  
> * Cyclical unemployment goes away when the economy is booming. But in Spain and South Africa, unemployment stays very high even during good economic years!  
> * In **Spain**, they have a 'two-tier' labor market: older workers have permanent contracts with big protections, while young people only get temporary short contracts and get fired first. Also, after the housing crash, construction workers did not have the skills for modern jobs.  
> * In **South Africa**, it is because of the history of Apartheid: Black workers were forced to live in distant areas far from city jobs, so transport is too expensive. Also, their school system lacks technical training, creating a huge skills mismatch."
