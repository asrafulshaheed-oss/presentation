## 🕒 Minute 1:55 to 3:55 — SLIDE 4: Shocks, Recovery & Structural Gaps
*(Click to Slide 4. Speak with confidence. Point to each panel and chip as you speak.)*

> "Slide 4 answers three questions.  
> 
> **Question 1: Who was hit hardest?**  
> * In **2008**, **Spain** `[point to 2008 panel]`. Unemployment went from **8% to 26%**, because the housing and construction bubble burst. The US went from **4.6% to almost 10%**.  
> * **Germany** had almost **no increase**, thanks to **Kurzarbeit** `[say: KOORTZ-ar-bite]`. Companies cut working hours instead of firing people, and the government paid part of the salary.  
> * **Japan** stayed low too. Many Japanese workers have **lifetime employment**: a secure job until retirement. Companies feel a duty to keep them. Also, the government's **Employment Adjustment Subsidy** pays part of the wages when workers stay home or train.  
> * In **2020** `[point to COVID panel]`, the **US** had the fastest jump: **14.7%** in April. But **South Africa** was hit hardest in total: a record **34%**.  
> 
> **Question 2: Who recovered?** `[point to the three chips]`  
> * The **US, Germany, and Japan** came back fast. **South Africa never recovered.** Spain took more than ten years, and it is still above **11%**.  
> 
> **Question 3: Why are Spain and South Africa so high?**  
> * Our answer: **structural, not cyclical.** `[point to red badge]`  
> * **Cyclical** unemployment goes up in a recession and down in a boom. **Structural** means workers and jobs do not match, so it stays high even in good years. Look: in 2007, its best year, Spain was still about **8%**. South Africa was above **20%** in most years. `[pause]`  
> * **Spain** has a **two-tier labor market**. Older workers have permanent, protected jobs. Young workers get short contracts and are fired first. Also, after the construction bubble burst, those workers lacked skills for modern jobs.  
> * In **South Africa**, **Apartheid** `[say: uh-PAR-tide]` forced Black workers to live far from city jobs, so transport is very expensive. Also, schools give little technical training. Jobs exist, but the skills do not match."
