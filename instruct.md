## 🧭 PART 1: The 5 Golden Rules for Speaking (English as 2nd Language)

### Rule 1: Speak 20% Slower Than Normal
* When people get nervous, their heart beats faster and they speak too fast. When you speak too fast in a second language, words get jumbled.
* **Coach's Tip:** Take a slow, deep breath before you say your first word. Aim for **120 words per minute**. If it feels slightly slow to you, it sounds *perfect and professional* to the audience!

### Rule 2: Short Sentences Are Your Best Friend
* Never try to speak long, complex sentences with multiple clauses.
* Speak in short, punchy sentences:  
  * *Instead of:* "Although we might conventionally think machines create mass joblessness, historical empirical data reveals this is completely inaccurate..."
  * *Say:* "Many people fear machines destroy jobs. But history shows the opposite. In fact, total jobs grew."
* Short sentences prevent you from getting lost mid-sentence.

### Rule 3: The "Silent Pause" Instead of "Ummm"
* Never say: *"Uhhhh... ummm... like... basically... you know..."*
* When you need a second to remember your next point, **just close your mouth and stay completely silent for 2 seconds**.
* To the professor, a 2-second silence looks like you are thinking deeply and showing maturity!

### Rule 4: Pronunciation Cheat-Sheet (Economic Terms)
Here are the only tricky words in your presentation. Practice saying them out loud 5 times:
* **Kurzarbeit** &rarr; Pronounce it: *“KOORTZ-ar-bite”* (German short-time work scheme).
* **Hysteresis** &rarr; Pronounce it: *“Hiss-ter-EE-sis”* (when temporary unemployment becomes permanent).
* **Polanyi** &rarr; Pronounce it: *“Po-LAN-yee”* (Michael Polanyi, philosopher).
* **Complementarity** &rarr; Pronounce it: *“Com-ple-men-TAIR-i-tee”* (machines helping workers).
* **Empirical** &rarr; Pronounce it: *“Em-PEER-i-kul”* (based on real data).

### Rule 5: Keep Your Voice Loud and Energetic
* Speak to the **back wall of the classroom**. If the student sitting in the very last row can hear you clearly, the professor in the front will hear you effortlessly.

## 👥 PART 2: Stage Presence & Body Language (The 3-Member Setup)

How you stand on stage communicates 50% of your grade before you even speak!

```
                    [ PROJECTOR SCREEN / SLIDES ]
                                  
                   Member 1        Member 2        Member 3
                   (Speaker)       (Teammate)      (Teammate)
                  [Steps Forward] [Stands Ready]  [Stands Ready]
                                       
                                 🎤
                                       
                           👨‍🏫 PROFESSOR
                            👥 AUDIENCE
```

### 1. The "Active Speaker" vs. "Support Members"
* **When it is YOUR turn to speak:** Take two confident steps forward toward the podium or audience. You own the stage.
* **When your teammate is speaking:** Stand straight, hands loosely clasped in front or at your side. 
  * ⚠️ **NEVER look at your phone, look at the floor, or lean on a wall.**
  * Look at your speaking teammate and nod occasionally. This shows the professor that your team is united and listening to each other!

### 2. Where Do Your Hands Go?
* Do **NOT** put your hands inside your pant pockets (looks casual/nervous).
* Do **NOT** cross your arms over your chest (looks defensive).
* **Do this:** Keep your hands open at waist level. Use your open hands to emphasize points or gesture gently toward the slide.

### 3. How to Point at the Screen
* When talking about a chart (Plot 1 or Plot 2), turn your head toward the screen for 2 seconds, point with an **open hand** (not an aggressive single finger), and say:  
  *"As you can see here in the red line for Spain..."*  
* Then immediately turn your eyes back to the audience! Never talk to the wall with your back to the audience.

### 4. The "3-Zone Eye Contact" Trick
* Divide the room into 3 zones:
  1. Left side of the classroom.
  2. The Professor in the middle.
  3. Right side of the classroom.
* Look at each zone for 4 to 5 seconds, then smoothly move to the next. This makes everyone feel included.

The professor will stop you at **exactly 12 minutes**. If Member 1 takes 6 minutes, Member 3 will get stopped before finishing and will lose presentation marks!

| Presenter | Start Time | Finish Time | Target Time | Hard Stop Alert |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |

| **Member 2** | **4:00** | **7:45** | 3 min 45 sec | If timer hits 7:30, jump straight to the handoff to Member 3! |

### How to Track Time on Stage:
* Put one mobile phone flat on the podium or table with the **Stopwatch running silently**.
* Keep the screen brightness high. Every speaker can glance down and see the exact seconds without looking awkward.

## 🤝 PART 4: Seamless Team Handoffs

Never say: *"Okay, my part is done, now he will talk."* (Sounds unprepared).  
Use these professional, smooth transitions:

### Handoff 1: Member 1 to Member 2 (At 3:45)
* **Member 1 turns to Member 2, smiles, and gestures:**  
  > *"To explain whether technology caused long-run unemployment in the US, and to introduce David Autor's theory, I now invite my teammate, Member 2. Member 2, the floor is yours."*
* **Member 2 takes two steps forward, smiles, and says:**  
  > *"Thank you, Member 1. Good morning/afternoon everyone, I am Member 2..."*

### Handoff 2: Member 2 to Member 3 (At 7:45)
* **Member 2 turns to Member 3, smiles, and gestures:**  
  > *"Now, while technology creates new jobs, it also squeezes the middle class and brings challenges for developing countries. To discuss job polarization, AI, and Bangladesh, I hand over to Member 3."*
* **Member 3 takes two steps forward, smiles, and says:**  
  > *"Thank you, Member 2. Respected faculty and friends, I am Member 3..."*

## 🆘 PART 5: Emergency Protocols (What If Something Goes Wrong?)

### Scenario A: You Blank Out / Forget Your Next Sentence
1. **Do not say:** *"Sorry, I forgot."*
2. **Do this:** Take a slow breath. Look up at the slide title. Read the bullet point on the slide out loud!  
   Say: *"Looking at our second key point here on the slide..."*  
   Reading the slide title out loud gives your brain 3 seconds to recover and remember your speech.

### Scenario B: A Teammate Completely Freezes for More Than 7 Seconds
* This is where team marks are saved!
* The teammate standing closest steps up gently, smiles at the professor, and says:  
  *"And to add to what my teammate was explaining, this chart shows that..."*  
* Finish that one point, then hand the speech back to your teammate: *"Member 1, please continue."*  
* Professors give huge bonus respect to teams that support each other gracefully!

## 🛡️ PART 6: Winning the 3-Minute Q&A Defense (3 Marks)

Remember the rule: *"Anyone can be asked any question, so everyone must understand the whole presentation."*

### The 3-Step Formula to Answer Any Question:
1. **Step 1 (Buy 2 seconds of time):**  
   *"Thank you, sir, for this important question."* (or *"That is a very good question."*)
2. **Step 2 (Give the direct answer in 1 clear sentence):**  
   *"Yes, this unemployment is structural because it lasts even during economic booms."*
3. **Step 3 (Give 1 supporting reason or example):**  
   *"For example, in Spain, workers who lost construction jobs after 2008 did not have the skills needed for modern tech or service jobs."*

### Who Jumps In First:
* **Questions about 2008 crisis, Spain, Germany, or South Africa:** &rarr; **Member 1** answers first.
* **Questions about Long-run US data, Lump of labor, ATMs, or O-Ring theory:** &rarr; **Member 2** answers first.
* **Questions about Job polarization, Polanyi's paradox, AI, or Bangladesh RMG:** &rarr; **Member 3** answers first.
* If the teacher points directly to one specific member, that member answers first, and teammates can chime in with extra details after 20 seconds.

## ⭐ PART 7: Stealing the +2 Individual Bonus Marks!

The syllabus gives up to **2 bonus marks per person** for asking good questions to OTHER groups presenting on GDP, Inflation, or Growth!

### How to Win Your +2 Bonus Marks:
1. As soon as another group says: *"Any questions?"*, **immediately be the first person to shoot your hand up!**
2. Stand up, speak clearly:  
   *"Thank you for your presentation. I am [Your Name], ID: [Your ID]. I have a quick macroeconomic question for the group..."*
3. Read one of the ready-made questions from `Bonus_Marks_Question_Bank.md`:
   * **If the group presented on GDP:**  
     > *"In your GDP chart, does that measure market exchange rate or Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)? Because in countries like Bangladesh, market exchange rates undervalue real living standards."*
   * **If the group presented on Inflation:**  
     > *"Was the inflation spike in your chart caused by Demand-Pull inflation from government stimulus, or Cost-Push inflation from supply chain problems?"*
   * **If the group presented on Growth:**  
     > *"According to the Solow Growth Model, capital accumulation has diminishing returns. How much of your country's long-run growth was driven by technology (TFP) versus just adding more machines?"*
4. The professor will write down your ID on their grade sheet immediately. That is an instant **+2 individual bonus marks** in your pocket!
